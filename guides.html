Build Guides

Two builds worth trying tonight.

Dripstone Lava Farm

Works on Java and Bedrock. One lava bucket per column lasts forever — the source is never consumed.

Per column: 1 cauldron, 1 pointed dripstone, 1 lava bucket, 2 solid blocks.

  1. Place a cauldron on the ground.
  2. Leave one air block above it, then place a solid block (2 blocks above the cauldron).
  3. Attach a pointed dripstone to the underside of that block, tip pointing down, hanging into the air block directly above the cauldron.
  4. Put a lava source block on top of the solid block. It must be a source, not flowing, directly above the block the dripstone hangs from.
  5. Wait. Lava drips from the stalactite tip into the cauldron. When full, scoop it out with an empty bucket — and it starts refilling on its own.

Why it's worth it: a lava bucket smelts 100 items, the best furnace fuel in the game, and you get the bucket back. Lava drips much slower than water, so build a row of 6 to 10 columns side by side and let it run in the background. Unlike the water version, the lava farm works in the Nether too.

Dripstone itself comes from dripstone caves, or occasionally from the wandering trader.

Quasi-Connectivity 2x2 Piston Door

Java only. Bedrock pistons don't do quasi-connectivity, so the top pistons would never fire there. On Java, this is the most compact 2x2 door there is.

You need: 4 sticky pistons, 2 redstone torches, redstone dust, 4 pressure plates, 4 door blocks (anything solid, ideally matching your wall).

  1. Dig a trench under where the door will be: 2 wide, 3 long, 2 deep. This hides the wiring.
  2. On each side of the trench, dig out one extra block and place a redstone torch on the side of a block there, one block above the trench floor.
  3. Place a solid block on top of each torch, then put a sticky piston on top of that block, facing the doorway. The torch powers the block above it, which powers the piston — so the piston extends. This is the bottom piston on each side.
  4. Stack a second sticky piston on top of each bottom piston, also facing the doorway.
  5. Place a block on top of each top piston, and put redstone dust on top of those blocks. This dust is the QC link — it powers the block below it, and the top piston reads power from the block above it even though nothing touches it directly.
  6. Place your 4 door blocks in the 2x2 gap. The extended pistons push them into place.
  7. Place pressure plates on the floor on both sides of the door (2 per side, covering the walkway).

Why it works: the torches are always on, so the pistons stay extended and the door stays closed. When you step on a plate, it powers the block the torch is attached to, which turns the torch off — pistons lose power, retract, and the door opens. Step off, the torch comes back on, and the door closes behind you.

Two beginner traps: it has to be sticky pistons — regular pistons push the blocks out but won't pull them back. And stone plates only trigger for players and mobs, while wooden plates also trigger from a dropped item.

Tip: build it once in a throwaway creative world before putting it in your base. Redstone is much easier to debug when you can see the wiring.

Java and Bedrock unless noted. The piston door is Java-only (quasi-connectivity doesn't exist on Bedrock). Creaking needs Java 1.21.4+ / Bedrock 1.21.50+; happy ghasts need Java 1.21.6+ / Bedrock 1.21.90+.
